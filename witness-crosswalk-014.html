Conditioned calm is not the absence of grief.

Linda dies in the Park Lane Hospital for the Dying. John grieves. The hospital staff attend to a children's death-conditioning program, and the children's behavior reflects their training. The institution's educational objective does not settle John's grief, Linda's dignity, or the harm of physical aggression toward children.

John and Linda

John visits Linda, recalls their shared years, reacts to her confusion, fears her death and blames himself. Linda's final condition and John's guilt are separate evidence roots. His feeling of responsibility is not a clinical causal determination.

Observed: Linda dies; John is distressed.

The ward and children

The nurse supervises the children's exposure to the dying and uses chocolate éclairs to restore positive feelings after John's display. Children's calm behavior does not demonstrate a mature understanding of death or negate the bereaved person's experience.

Observed: a managed conditioning routine.
